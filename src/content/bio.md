Broadly, I'm interested in the <em class="hl hl-1">science of (mis)alignment</em> ([Young, 2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/FogmcDHA6AdMGukum/alignment-engineering-vs-misalignment-science)). Within it, I focus on how <strong class="hl hl-1">multi-agent AI systems</strong> exhibit failure modes that are invisible to single-agent evaluation — emergent collusion, cross-agent goal drift, deceptive coordination — and on building infrastructure that can monitor and control them. The work runs along three threads: <em class="hl hl-1">studying failure</em> itself, especially the misalignment that emerges under conditions where standard evaluation looks clean ([Betley et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17424)); <em class="hl hl-1">studying mechanistically</em>, on what these systems are actually doing inside when they coordinate, deceive, or drift; and <em class="hl hl-1">agents and society</em>, on how multi-agent systems get embedded in human institutions, the technical analysis of that coexistence ([Reuel et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14981)), and catastrophic risk threat modelling.

Currently a second-year Master's student in CS at **Dartmouth College**, co-advised by Soroush Vosoughi ([Minds, Machines, and Society Lab](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~soroush/)) and Nikhil Singh ([Science and Art of Human-AI Systems Lab](https://www.sahaslab.com/)). Before this, I did my undergrad at **New York University** in Computer Science and Mathematics, and undergrad thesis with [Multimodal Agentic Personalization Systems (MAPS) Group](https://whongyi.github.io/MAPS-research).
